Physical Symptoms of Anxiety People Often Miss

By Abhinaw • 22 April 2026 • 6 min read

Please read first: every symptom described here can also be caused by physical medical conditions. If you have new or worrying physical symptoms, especially chest pain or breathing difficulty, get checked by a doctor first. Anxiety should be a conclusion reached after medical causes are considered, not an assumption made instead of it.

Many people arrive at counselling saying some version of: "I did not think I was anxious, I thought something was wrong with my body." They had been to physicians, had tests run, and been told the results were normal.

This is common, and it is not imagination. Anxiety is a whole-body event, not just a mood.

Why Anxiety Is Physical

When your brain registers a threat, it prepares you to deal with it. Heart rate rises to move blood to muscles. Breathing quickens. Digestion is deprioritised, because digesting lunch is not the priority during an emergency. Muscles tense.

This system is efficient and useful. The difficulty is that it responds to a difficult email much as it responds to physical danger, and when it stays partly switched on for weeks or months, you feel the effects in your body.

Signs That Are Easy to Miss

  • Jaw tension and teeth grinding. Often noticed first by a dentist, or as morning headaches.
  • Digestive trouble. Nausea, cramping, appetite changes, or urgency. The gut and brain are closely linked.
  • Shoulder and neck pain. Long-held bracing that people attribute entirely to posture or their desk.
  • A tight chest, or feeling unable to take a satisfying breath. Frequently the symptom that sends people to a cardiologist.
  • Dizziness or a floaty, unreal feeling. Often related to subtle over-breathing that people do not notice they are doing.
  • Tingling in hands, feet or face. Also linked to breathing pattern changes.
  • Exhaustion that sleep does not fix. Sustained vigilance is metabolically expensive.
  • Frequent urination, or a permanently unsettled stomach.
  • Trouble concentrating or a sense of mental fog. Attention is occupied with threat-scanning.

The Loop That Keeps It Going

Physical symptoms of anxiety often become their own trigger. You notice your heart racing. You wonder whether something is wrong with your heart. That worry activates the stress response further, which raises your heart rate again, which confirms the fear.

Understanding this loop is often the single most useful thing a person learns. The sensation is real, and it is not evidence of danger.

What Helps

Get the medical reassurance once, properly. Then work on trusting it. Repeated checking and repeated tests tend to feed the cycle rather than settle it.

Work with your breathing. Slow, low breathing with a longer exhale addresses several of these symptoms directly, because a good number of them come from breathing pattern rather than from the original worry.

Discharge the activation. The stress response prepares your body for movement. Walking, stretching or any physical activity gives that preparation somewhere to go.

Notice the tension you are holding. Jaw, shoulders, hands. Many people hold these braced all day without registering it.

Address the source, not only the symptoms. Breathing techniques manage the moment. If the underlying pressure is unchanged, the symptoms return. This is usually where talking it through with someone helps most.

When to Seek Support

If physical symptoms have been medically investigated and are continuing, if they are limiting what you do, or if you find yourself organising your life around avoiding them, that is a reasonable point to speak to someone.

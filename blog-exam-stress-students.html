Exam Stress and Student Anxiety

11 August 2026 • 6 min read

Exam pressure in India is not ordinary academic stress. Between board exams, competitive entrances, coaching schedules and the sense that a single result determines your entire future, students carry a load that would test most adults.

For Students

Some stress helps. Too much does not. A moderate level sharpens focus. Past a point it does the opposite, impairing exactly the memory and reasoning you need. If you are studying for ten hours and retaining little, more hours is not the solution.

Sleep is study time. Memory consolidation happens during sleep. Cutting sleep to add revision hours is, mechanically, trading the process that stores information for more time spent inputting it. Regularly sleeping four hours before an exam period tends to lower marks, not raise them.

Test yourself instead of re-reading. Re-reading notes feels productive because the material becomes familiar. Familiarity is not recall. Practising retrieval, closing the book and writing what you remember, is harder and considerably more effective.

Study in defined blocks with real breaks. Sustained concentration has limits. Blocks of around 40 to 50 minutes with genuine breaks between them beat an unbroken six-hour session that quietly becomes four hours of low-quality attention.

Stop comparing timetables. Someone will always claim more hours. It tells you nothing about their retention, and comparison raises anxiety while doing nothing for your preparation.

Keep some movement. Even a short daily walk protects concentration and sleep. It is not lost study time.

On the Day

  • Blanking usually passes. Put the pen down, breathe out slowly for a few breaths, start with a question you can answer.
  • Do not review notes in the last ten minutes. It raises panic without adding knowledge.
  • Avoid post-mortems with friends immediately afterwards. It changes nothing and damages the next paper.

For Parents

Your anxiety transmits. Children read parental tension accurately, even when nothing is said. Managing your own worry about their result is part of supporting them.

Ask about them, not only about preparation. If every conversation for six months opens with "how much have you finished," the message received is that your regard depends on output.

Be careful with comparison. Mentioning a cousin's rank rarely motivates. It usually lands as: I am a disappointment.

Say the thing directly. Many students genuinely believe their parents' love is conditional on results. Saying plainly that it is not, and that they matter more than the outcome, is worth more than it sounds.

Protect the basics. Sleep, food, some downtime. You have more influence over these than over hours studied.

Signs That Need Attention

Watch for withdrawal from everyone, sleep or appetite changes that persist, panic symptoms, statements of hopelessness, expressions of worthlessness, or any mention of not wanting to be here. Student distress in India is a serious issue and these signs should never be dismissed as ordinary exam nerves.

If a student is expressing thoughts of self-harm or suicide, act immediately. Stay with them, remove access to means, and get professional help involved. Call the KIRAN helpline on 1800-599-0019, Tele-MANAS on 14416, or emergency services on 112. Talking openly about it does not plant the idea. It opens a door.

The Longer View

One examination does not determine a life. It genuinely does not, however convincingly the surrounding environment insists otherwise. There are many routes, many second attempts and many people whose eventual work bore little relation to a result at seventeen. That perspective is nearly impossible to hold from inside the pressure, which is exactly why talking to someone outside it helps.

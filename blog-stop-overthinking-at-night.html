How to Stop Overthinking at Night

By Abhinaw • 8 April 2026 • 6 min read

You were fine all day. You get into bed, switch off the light, and suddenly your mind opens every file it has been holding shut since morning. Conversations. Deadlines. That thing you said in 2019.

This is one of the most common experiences people bring to counselling, and it is not a sign that something is wrong with you.

Why Night Is Worse

Three things stack up after dark.

The distractions stop. All day, work, traffic, people and phones occupy your attention. Thoughts that were waiting in the queue finally get the floor when everything else goes quiet.

Your body is winding down but your nervous system has not caught up. If you have been running on stress hormones all day, lying still does not automatically switch them off. You end up physically still and mentally accelerated.

Tired brains are worse at perspective. Fatigue reduces your capacity to regulate emotion, so problems that felt manageable at 2pm feel enormous at 1am. The problem did not grow. Your resources shrank.

What to Do Before Bed

Do a brain dump, on paper, an hour before sleep. Write everything unfinished. Tasks, worries, half-thoughts. The goal is not to solve anything, it is to tell your mind the list exists somewhere other than inside it.

Close the day deliberately. A short, boring, repeated routine signals to your body that the day is over. Same order, same time. Predictability is what makes it work, not the specific activity.

Stop scrolling earlier than feels necessary. Not only for the light. Feeds deliver a steady stream of small emotional hits, and your mind keeps processing them after you put the phone down.

What to Do at 2am

Lengthen your exhale. Breathe in for four counts, out for six or eight. A longer out-breath nudges the nervous system toward its calming branch. Keep it going for a few minutes rather than a few breaths.

Get out of bed after about twenty minutes. This feels counterproductive but matters. Lying awake teaches your brain that bed is a place for thinking. Sit somewhere dim and dull until you feel sleepy, then return.

Give the thought a time slot. Silently answer the loop with "not now, tomorrow at 10." You are not dismissing the concern, you are declining the meeting request at this hour.

Drop into your senses. Name five things you can hear. Feel the weight of the blanket. Attention cannot fully occupy the body and the spiral at the same time.

Stop checking the clock. Every check adds arithmetic and pressure. Turn it away from you.

The Thing Most People Miss

Night-time overthinking is often a symptom of a day with no processing space in it. If your waking hours are fully packed from alarm to pillow, the mind takes the only unscheduled slot available. Building even fifteen unstructured minutes into your day, with no input and no screen, often does more for night thoughts than anything you do at bedtime.

When to Get Support

Consider reaching out if sleep difficulty has continued for several weeks, if you are exhausted but still cannot switch off, or if the thoughts at night carry significant dread. Persistent sleep problems can also have physical causes, so it is worth mentioning them to a doctor as well.
